01 · The instrument

Four independent questions, 70 minutes, all visible at once — so the order is yours to choose

This is a different instrument from the Anthropic CodeSignal assessment, and confusing the two is the fastest way to prepare wrong. Anthropic's is an Industry Coding Assessment: one problem, four gated levels, your own code carried forward. Capital One's is the General Coding Assessment (GCA): four unrelated questions, nothing carried forward, nothing gated. Design-for-the-next-level discipline earns you nothing here; triage and speed earn everything.

Does clicking “Start Assessment” start the clock?

Almost certainly not — but do not test it. The button on mycareeratcapitalone.com is a router: it hands you to whichever vendor that role uses. What follows is a setup flow — identity verification, camera and microphone permission, screen share, close-your-other-apps — and the timer starts on a second, explicit start button after all of that. Where Capital One uses Codility, the vendor says so outright: you may open the invite, read the intro, the policies, the task count and the time limit, then leave and come back.

The asymmetry is what decides this, not the odds. Being right costs you nothing; being wrong costs the attempt. There is nothing behind that button you want to see early. Click it only when you have the full uninterrupted block free — and if you want certainty rather than inference, your invitation email states the time limit and whether the session is resumable.

FORMAT

Four questions, 70 minutes

Independent problems, mixed easy/medium/hard. All four are visible the moment the timer starts, and you allocate the 70 minutes yourself.

SCORE

300–850

Correctness, speed and implementation quality. 300 is the floor for submitting nothing, so a partial attempt at every question strictly beats leaving one blank.

ORDER

Not sequential — triage first

Spend the first three minutes reading all four and ranking them by points-per-minute. Multiple candidate reports put Q4 as easier than Q3 at the same weight.

REUSE

The score is portable

A GCA score attaches to your CodeSignal profile and is commonly accepted by other GCA employers for ~6 months. You are not sitting this only for Capital One.

The one structural fact worth more than any algorithm

Because all four are visible and scoring is cumulative, this assessment rewards abandonment. The failure mode is not "couldn't solve the hard one" — it is sinking thirty minutes into one problem and leaving two easy ones untouched. Set a hard per-question ceiling before you start, and when you hit it, submit whatever passes and move. A brute-force solution that passes the small cases scores; an elegant half-written one does not.

02 · Drills

The four archetypes, and your drill file

Reported question categories are stable even though the exact problems rotate. Each row below has a worked, self-testing drill in capital-one-gca-solutions.py — run it and it must exit clean.

ArchetypeWhat it looks likeBudgetDrill
String / array basics Deliberately easy. Points are lost to spec-reading, not difficulty — "first-seen order", "after trimming", "case-insensitive" are where they hide. 8 min normalize_account_tags
Reading-heavy conversion A long spec, simple operations, one rule that interacts with the others. Banking and ledger framing is common here for obvious reasons. 15 min replay_ledger
Matrix implementation No clever algorithm — pure boundary bookkeeping. The bug is always the last row or column, emitted twice or not at all. 18 min spiral_sums
Optimization The one question where the naive loop times out. In practice this is a monotonic stack or a prefix-sum hashmap far more often than it is anything exotic. 20 min days_until_higher_rate, count_balanced_windows

The two patterns that carry the optimization question

Monotonic stack — push indices, not values, or you need a second scan to find where they came from. Prefix-sum hashmap — seed {0: 1} (the empty prefix, which is what lets a window starting at index 0 count) and look up before inserting (or a window matches itself when the target is 0). Both live in string-array-patterns.py alongside the other six, each with the exact token that breaks it.

03 · Test day

Before you click

  1. Read the invitation email for the real time limit. Everything on this page is the reported norm; your email is authoritative and this page is not.
  2. One screen, nothing else open. Same proctoring discipline as any proctored CodeSignal sitting — the assessment is screen-shared and camera-on, and a second monitor is a flag.
  3. Pick your language before the timer, not after. Python unless the employer restricts it; know your own collections imports cold (Counter, defaultdict, deque) because there is no IDE and no autocomplete worth trusting.
  4. Three minutes of triage before the first keystroke. Rank all four, write the order down, set the per-question ceilings.
  5. Submit something for every question. Brute force that passes small cases beats an unfinished optimum, and 300 is what a blank scores.

04 · Confidence

What to trust, and what comes next

Confidence — read this before you rely on any number above

The 4-questions / 70-minutes / 300–850 format is CodeSignal's own documented GCA structure and is solid. The advancement thresholds circulating for Capital One (roughly 750+ general, 800+ for software engineering, 700–730 for analyst tracks) are community self-reports, not published figures, and a meaningful share of the pages repeating them are AI-generated SEO content citing each other. Treat them as directional only. The retake limits (2 per 30 days, 3 per 6 months) and the ~6-month score validity are CodeSignal platform policy rather than a Capital One rule. Anything that actually gates you — the time limit, the language list, the deadline — is in your invitation email or answerable by your recruiter, and that is the source to trust over this page.

If you clear it, the next stage is Capital One's Power Day: technical coding, system design, a case study and behavioural, back to back in one day. The coding round there is reported to be class-based/OOP design rather than LeetCode-style, which is a different preparation again — worth its own section if you get there.

00 · Read this first

Three points, then get the details

1 · You own systems at scale

Papercusp runs about a hundred AI agents against one production system. That means concurrency, crash recovery, durable workflows, a release gate, and observability. These are the same problems Coupang's back-end teams handle.

2 · You know commerce from the inside

You ran a commerce business for twelve years: over $1M a month with a very small team. You built your own pricing model over marketplace listings. You've seen marketplaces, inventory, and fulfillment as a seller, which is exactly what Rocket Growth serves.

3 · Staff means ownership

You set the direction, made the technical calls, and were on call. Use the word "ownership," and give one concrete outcome.

Get these four answers before you hang up: (1) which team and hiring manager, (2) location and in-office expectation, (3) the next step: Coupang lists a one-hour technical screen with 20–30 minutes of live coding, (4) the timeline.

01 · What you're interviewing for

Coupang in a minute

Coupang is South Korea's largest online retailer, often called "the Amazon of Korea." It is listed on the NYSE as CPNG. It owns its logistics network, which makes Rocket Delivery (next-day, often same-day) possible, and it also operates in Taiwan. US engineering hubs are in Seattle and Mountain View.

Open Staff Back-end roles it could be

  • Rocket Growth (Seattle), posted 18 Sept. Coupang's marketplace and fulfillment service for third-party sellers. Large-scale back end and microservices, scalability, reliability. Observability: distributed tracing, OpenTelemetry, chaos engineering, incident leadership. 7+ years. $152K–$261K base.
  • Global Ops Tech, Picking (Seattle), posted 4 Sept. Fulfillment-center picking workflows; tech lead on cross-team Inventory and Promise projects. AWS; Java a plus.
  • Others: Search and Discovery (Mountain View), plus roles abroad.

What the stack likely looks like

Java microservices on AWS is the common thread in these postings. Be honest that your recent work is TypeScript, Node, Python, and Postgres. Say you've worked across languages and the hard parts carry over: consistency, failure handling, and observability.

The likely process (from Coupang's site and candidate reports)
  1. Recruiter screen: today.
  2. Technical phone screen, about an hour: coding, CS fundamentals, and some discussion of design. Some staff candidates report LP questions here too.
  3. Virtual loop of 4–5 rounds: coding, system design, hiring manager, and an Amazon-style "Bar Raiser." Behavioral questions are scored against Coupang's 15 leadership principles.

Candidate reports vary by team and country; ask Arpita for the exact loop.

What she's screening for

She decides whether to pass you to a hiring manager. She's checking that your level is Staff, that your story is clear, that you're motivated, and that location and compensation fit. She won't grade code. Keep answers to about a minute, use plain language, and give numbers.

03 · About 60 seconds

"Tell me about yourself"

"For the last year I've been building Papercusp, a platform where about a hundred AI agents work on one production codebase at the same time. I'm open-sourcing it next month. Most of the work was back-end systems: concurrency control so agents don't overwrite each other, durable workflows that survive crashes, a release gate that decides what ships, and observability to understand what's happening. Before that I ran a commerce business for twelve years, doing over a million a month with a very small team. I built our own pricing model over marketplace listings and owned the systems end to end, on-call included. Coupang is where both halves meet: commerce at huge scale, where reliability decides whether a delivery arrives on time. That's the kind of system I want to own."

Why Coupang: it does commerce at scale and runs its own logistics. Back-end engineering there directly affects whether customers get their orders, which is the side of the business you know.

Why now: Papercusp proved what you can build. Now you want to own large systems on a strong team, rather than start another company.

05 · Answers to rehearse

What a recruiter will ask

"What are your compensation expectations?"

"I'm targeting something in line with the posted range for Staff in my location, and I'm flexible on the mix of base and equity. I'd like to learn more about the level and the team before giving an exact number." If she pushes, give a range near the top of the posted band for your location. Don't name a number below it.

"Where are you located? Are you open to Seattle or Mountain View, or to coming into an office?"

Give your real answer plainly: where you are now, and whether you'd relocate or commute. Then ask whether this team allows remote work or requires a hub office.

"Have you used Java?"

Answer honestly, then pivot: "My recent production work is TypeScript and Python on Postgres. The hard parts of back-end work carry over between languages: consistency, retries, failure handling, observability. I'd be productive in Java quickly."

"Why are you leaving Papercusp?"

"Papercusp proved the ideas, and I'm open-sourcing it. I want to own large systems with real customers on a strong team, rather than also run a company."

"Are you interviewing elsewhere? What's your timeline?"

"Yes, I'm in a few processes, some in later stages. Coupang is a strong fit, so I'd like to keep this moving." That's honest and creates gentle urgency. You don't need to name companies.

"What makes you Staff level?"

Scope and judgment: you set the technical direction and made the architecture decisions, you owned production, and you wrote decisions down so others could follow them. Give one example, like the release gate or durable workflows.

06 · For later rounds, and maybe today

Coupang's leadership principles

Coupang says its 15 principles drive how candidates are assessed. They closely mirror Amazon's, so prepare stories in Situation, Task, Action, Result (STAR) format. The full list below comes from a 2021 forum post comparing them with Amazon's, so treat the exact wording as unverified.

PrincipleYour story
Wow the CustomerYour commerce business: what customers needed, and something you changed because of it.
Company-wide PerspectiveFixing a red release gate even when it wasn't your change, because it blocks everyone.
Dive DeepThe metric that measured the wrong set of test runs: you traced the number back to the code that produced it before trusting it.
Simplify / Hate WasteRunning a business over $1M a month with a very small team; automating instead of hiring.
Deliver Results with GritCrash-safe durable workflows: you defined what may replay and verified it with a real crash test.
Disagree and Commit / Influence without AuthorityRecording rulings as written decisions with reasons, so everyone works from the source rather than someone's paraphrase.

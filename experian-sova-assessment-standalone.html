01 · Read this first

Three rules carry the whole assessment

RULE 1 / HONESTY

Answer the personality items consistently

It is not pass/fail; it is scored as competency profiles. Sova's forced-choice format asks the same traits several ways, so an answer set tuned to “what they want” comes out inconsistent, and inconsistency reads worse than any single trait.

RULE 2 / FORMAT

Learn the item types before the clock starts

Every cognitive item is a known shape: a table to read, a passage to judge, a shape sequence to complete. The knowledge is secondary-school level. The points are lost to unfamiliar formats, not hard content.

RULE 3 / PACING

Ninety seconds, then guess and move

There is no hard time limit, but Sova reports speed and accuracy together as one percentile. A question you sit on for four minutes costs more than a wrong answer. Never leave one blank.

HOW IT DIFFERS

Not CodeSignal, not an interview

No IDE, no hidden tests, no partial credit per case, nobody to talk to. The Kaseya page and the Answer Bank do not help here; the only transferable asset is a calm, well-rested twenty-five minutes.

What the flow looks like

Sova blends the sections into one sitting with short instructions between them. Expect the personality questionnaire, then (if Experian's configuration includes it) a handful of workplace scenarios, then the three cognitive blocks in some order. You can usually pause between blocks but not inside one. Read each block's instructions once, carefully; they state the response format and whether the block is timed.

02 · Behavioral half

Personality questionnaire, and possibly a situational judgment test

Personality questionnaire

  • Format. Sets of four statements. Rank each 1–5 from “least like me” to “most like me”, with no ties inside a set. There are typically several dozen sets; each takes fifteen to twenty seconds.
  • Scoring. Not pass/fail. The answers are mapped to competency profiles (drive, resilience, collaboration, analytical style) and compared to the role's target profile.
  • How to answer. As the engineer you actually are at work, quickly, first instinct. If two statements are both true of you, rank the one you would act on first.
  • The trap. Picking the “impressive” statement each time. The instrument asks each trait from several angles; a gamed profile disagrees with itself and is flagged as low-consistency.

Situational judgment, if present

  • Format. A short workplace scenario and four or five responses. Pick the most and the least effective. Sometimes rank all of them.
  • Anchor. Experian's stated values: integrity, collaboration, customer focus, and responsibility with data. Model the “most effective” answer on those, not on speed or heroics.
  • The reliable pattern. Most effective is usually gather the facts, communicate directly with the person involved, take ownership of the next step. Least effective is ignore it, escalate before trying, or go around a colleague.
  • Data scenarios. Anything touching customer data, access, or a shortcut around a control: the compliant option is the most effective one, even when it is slower.

Consistency beats polish

If a personality set forces you to rank “I prefer to work alone” against “I enjoy leading a group”, answer the way you would describe yourself to a colleague who knows you. The same pair will come back in different words; the second answer has to match the first without you having to remember it, and that only happens if both were true.

03 · Cognitive half

Numerical, verbal, logical: the three formats and the one trap in each

Numerical reasoning

  • Items. A table or chart, then a question: a percentage, a ratio, an average, a percent change, a projection from a trend.
  • Kit. Paper, pen, and a calculator on the desk before you start. Sova does not always provide an on-screen one.
  • Method. Read the question first, then find the two or three numbers it needs in the table. Do not read the whole table.
  • Trap. Units and bases: “percent change from 2023 to 2024” divides by the 2023 figure; “thousands” in a column header; a chart whose axis does not start at zero.

Verbal reasoning

  • Items. A short passage, then statements to mark true, false, or cannot say.
  • Rule. Judge only from the passage. “Cannot say” means the passage does not state it or imply it directly, however plausible it is in the real world.
  • Method. Read the statement, then scan the passage for its key noun. True requires an explicit match; false requires an explicit contradiction; everything else is cannot say.
  • Trap. Using outside knowledge. Engineers lose these on topics they know well, because the passage says less than they do.

Logical reasoning

  • Items. A sequence or grid of abstract shapes with one missing. Pick the item that completes the rule.
  • Rules to look for. Rotation (by a fixed angle), count (of sides, dots, or shapes), shading (alternating or cycling), position (moving around a frame), and size. Sova usually applies two rules at once.
  • Method. Name one rule out loud, eliminate the options that break it, then find the second rule among what is left.
  • Trap. Chasing a third rule when two already leave one option. Pick it and move.

Pacing is the score

Sova's own scoring note says the ability percentile combines the number correct and the time taken. A candidate who answers everything in fifteen minutes at 80% typically outranks one who takes thirty at 90%. Set a private budget of about ninety seconds per cognitive item; at the limit, choose the best remaining option and go on.

Adaptive or fixed?

Some Sova cognitive blocks adapt difficulty to your answers, so items getting harder is a good sign, not a bad one. Do not read difficulty as a verdict on how you are doing.

04 · Practice plan

About two hours in total, spread over two days

  1. Session 1 · 40 min · the official practice

    Sova's Candidate Preparation Hub: one pass through every item type it offers, untimed, reading each block's instructions in full. This is the only source that uses the exact formats you will see. Note which block felt slowest.

  2. Session 2 · 30 min · one free timed set

    One numerical and one verbal set from Practice Aptitude Tests or AssessmentDay, timed. Mark every miss as format, arithmetic, or outside knowledge. The third category is the one to fix; re-read the verbal rule in section 03.

  3. Session 3 · 20 min · the slow block again

    Whichever block was slowest in session 1, timed, at ninety seconds per item. Stop when you can hold the pace with most items correct.

  4. Session 4 · 15 min · read Experian's values and the day-of checklist

    Experian's careers page states its values; read them once so the situational judgment anchors are fresh. Then section 05 below. No personality practice: there is nothing to rehearse and rehearsing makes the profile less consistent.

  5. The day · 25 min

    Morning, rested, laptop, wired or strong Wi-Fi, phone in another room. One block at a time; do not stop in the middle of one.

05 · Day-of checklist

Ten things, none of them about ability

  • Laptop, not phone. Charger plugged in. Browser updated; pop-up blocker allowed for the Sova domain.
  • Quiet room, door closed, 40 uninterrupted minutes blocked even though the test is 25.
  • Paper, pen, and a physical calculator on the desk.
  • Water. No caffeine spike in the last hour; the personality half reads better calm.
  • Open the invite link once beforehand to confirm it loads and that the deadline shown matches what you were told.
  • Read each block's instructions completely. They state the format and whether the block is timed.
  • Personality: first instinct, no ties, answer as yourself at work.
  • Situational judgment: facts first, speak directly, own the next step; the compliant option wins on anything touching data.
  • Cognitive: question before table; passage only, never outside knowledge; two rules for shapes; ninety seconds then move.
  • Never leave an item blank. Never refresh the browser mid-block; use the platform's own navigation.

01 · What you're interviewing for

A wealth manager that builds its own platform

Farther pairs human financial advisors with a proprietary platform for account opening, planning and portfolio management, built in-house rather than stitched from vendors. It raised a $150M Series D led by General Atlantic in May 2026, at a reported valuation of over $1B, and reports more than $23B in recruited assets.

Company

Who runs it

Co-founder and CEO Taylor Matthews; co-founder and CTO Brad Genser, previously an AI team lead in Goldman Sachs' private wealth business. Headquartered in New York. Earlier investors include CapitalG (Series C, Oct 2024), Bessemer, Khosla and Lightspeed. Farther acquired Crooked Tree Capital Advisors in January 2026.

“$23B recruited assets” includes assets expected from joining advisors; it isn't the same as current AUM. Don't quote it as AUM.

The mandate

Own the patterns, make them the default

  • Access: API authentication and authorization, IAM policy design, account and network boundaries, secrets. Least privilege, every grant traceable.
  • Data protection: storage layout and segmentation, encryption in transit and at rest, KMS, retention.
  • Detect early: GuardDuty, Security Hub, Config, CloudTrail; SLIs, SLOs and dashboards with CloudWatch, X-Ray and RUM.
  • Compliance evidence: documented controls, automated evidence, audit logging.
  • Platform: secure-by-default shared services on ECS, Lambda, SQS/SNS, RDS and S3; infrastructure as code; design reviews.
What they stress

AI fluency with ownership

The posting asks for “fluent daily use of AI coding tools, paired with real ownership of the result: you can explain how anything you shipped works, defend the design choices, and say where it would fail.” That's your Papercusp story nearly word for word. Use it.

Positioning: you've owned production platforms end to end and built enforcement into the system rather than relying on review. The honest gap is AWS estate ownership. Your infrastructure has been GCP and your own Linux hosts. Name it early and bridge it with specifics; a recruiter will pass along a precise answer, and a vague one gets you filtered out.

04 · Likely recruiter topics · 30–60 seconds each

Answers to rehearse

Practice prompts, not a confirmed Farther script. Answer first, add one example, let them follow up.

Why Farther, and why this team?

“Two reasons. Farther builds its own platform, so infrastructure decisions directly shape what advisors and clients experience. And this team's job is to make access, data protection and monitoring the default for every other team. That's the part of engineering I enjoy most: building guardrails into the platform so other people move faster safely. Wealth management also raises the stakes in a way I find motivating: client data and money have to be handled correctly every time.”

Why join a company after running your own business and projects?

“I want to go deep on one important platform with a strong team, and spend my time on engineering rather than everything else a founder does. Running Store Wolf taught me to tie engineering decisions to business consequences. Building Papercusp taught me how to enforce standards across many contributors. Farther is at a scale where both matter.”

How deep is your AWS experience?

“Honestly, my production infrastructure has been on GCP and on Linux hosts I ran myself. On AWS I've built against SQS, SNS and S3, but I haven't owned an AWS account structure end to end. The security patterns I've implemented transfer directly. At Store Wolf, secrets are fetched at boot under the machine's own identity instead of a shared key; on AWS that's a task role with Secrets Manager. Builds are reproducible from an image and a script; on AWS that's an image plus Terraform or CDK. I'd ramp quickly, and I'd like to know how much AWS-specific depth you need on day one.”

If you hold an AWS certification or have other real AWS work, lead with it. Don't round “consumer” up to “owner.”

Tell me about securing a system you owned.

“At Store Wolf the legacy configs carried plaintext secrets. I couldn't fix everything at once without blocking every deploy, so I added a scanner to the bundle build that refuses any file with a new secret-shaped value, with a baseline that can only shrink. I stopped the live key from shipping in the bundle, and moved runtime secrets into a secret manager fetched under the VM's own service account. That fetch fails loudly, because a silent failure left the server hanging on a password prompt.”

Confirm which parts you wrote and which you directed before using “I.”

Have you worked with compliance (SOC 2, Reg S-P, GLBA)?

“Not in a regulated firm. I understand the shape: GLBA is the statute for protecting customer financial information, and for SEC-registered firms like an RIA it shows up as Reg S-P. The 2024 amendments added a written incident-response programme, oversight of service providers, and notifying affected customers within 30 days. My approach to compliance is the same as to reliability: automate the evidence so it's a by-product of normal operations, not a scramble before an audit.”

How do you use AI coding tools?

“Every day, heavily, and I own the result. I built a platform specifically to make AI-written work verifiable: who changed what, what tests proved it, and a reviewer who isn't the author. I can explain any design I've shipped, defend the trade-offs, and tell you where it would break. Your posting asks for exactly that, and it's how I work.”

Are you comfortable with on-call and incident response?

“Yes. I've been the person responsible when things broke. What I care about is fixing the class, not just the instance. My best example is a failure that went unnoticed for six hours because the watchdog depended on the system it was watching. I'd like to know the rotation and the typical page load.”

What compensation are you looking for?

“I didn't see a range in the posting. What's budgeted for this level in New York, and how are equity and bonus structured?” If pressed: “For this scope I'm targeting [your base range], depending on the full package.” Decide your number before the call.

New York law requires pay ranges in job ads for roles performed there, so it's reasonable to ask for it directly.

Hybrid in New York? Work authorization? Timeline?

Ask about the cadence first: “How many office days does the Infrastructure team keep?” Then give your actual location and commute or relocation plan. Your existing prep records US citizenship with no sponsorship needed; confirm that's still accurate. 05 · Pick one when asked · About 45 seconds

Three stories that translate to platform security

For a recruiter, keep each to the problem, what you did, and the lasting change. Save the depth for the engineers.

Secrets · Least privilege

Moving secrets out of the bundle without stopping deploys

“Legacy configs carried plaintext secrets, and a hard block would have frozen every deploy. So I added a ratchet: the bundle build refuses any new secret-shaped value, the baseline of known files can only shrink, and runtime secrets come from a secret manager under the machine's own identity. The deploy kept moving while the exposure could only go down.”

Store Wolf deploy/: known-plaintext-secrets.txt, fetch-secrets.sh, and the Sept 2026 commit that stopped shipping the live key.

Monitoring · Detective controls

A failure nothing alarmed on for six hours

“A background routine kept ‘running’ but stopped doing its job, and our watchdog didn't notice because it watched the wrong step. The fix was two rules: a watchdog must not depend on the thing it watches, and you alert on the outcome, not the activity. Same idea as detective controls: watch for drift from the standard, not just for errors.”

N9 · Incident.

Secure by default · Standards

Default-deny, so the safe path is the easy one

“In Papercusp, a tool that doesn't declare a permission gate is refused, not allowed by omission. Above that sit role allowlists, per-identity capability checks, and a floor no automated role can use, like reading raw secrets. New tools get the right posture without anyone remembering to add it. That's what I'd want for Farther's platform patterns: new services inherit least privilege by default.”

06 · Ten minutes of reading, for the next round

AWS security vocabulary from the posting

A recruiter won't test these, but the next round will. Know what each one is for, in one sentence.

Identity and access

  • IAM Identity Center: SSO for people across accounts.
  • Organizations + SCPs: guardrails no account can exceed.
  • Permission boundaries: a cap on what a role can be granted.
  • IAM Access Analyzer: finds external and unused access.

Data protection

  • KMS: customer-managed keys, key policies, envelope encryption.
  • S3: Block Public Access, bucket policies, Object Lock for write-once logs.
  • Secrets Manager: rotation; access via task or instance roles.
  • VPC endpoints: private paths to AWS services.

Detection and evidence

  • CloudTrail: who called which API; an org trail to a log-archive account.
  • GuardDuty: threat detection from logs.
  • Config: resource drift against rules.
  • Security Hub: aggregated findings against standards.
  • CloudWatch, X-Ray, RUM: metrics, traces, real-user monitoring.

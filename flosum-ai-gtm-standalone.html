What an SDR or BDR actually is

Start here

The job description assumes this vocabulary completely and never defines any of it. It is not complicated — it is a division of labour on a sales team — but every question you get will be phrased in it, so it has to be automatic before you record.

SDR (Sales Development Rep) and BDR (Business Development Rep) are the same job under two names. Some companies use BDR for outbound and SDR for inbound; many use them interchangeably. Flosum's posting uses both. The role is the front of the sales team: find or catch potential customers, have a short conversation to work out whether they are worth a salesperson's time, and book the meeting. An SDR does not close deals and does not negotiate. They hand qualified prospects to someone who does.

MarketingMarketo
Runs campaigns and the website. Produces leads — anyone who filled a form, downloaded something, or showed up. A lead marketing believes is worth calling is an MQL, a Marketing Qualified Lead.
SDR / BDROutreach, Qualified
Works the top of the funnel. Outbound: researches target accounts and reaches out cold across email, LinkedIn and phone in sequences. Inbound: responds fast to form fills and website chat. Either way they qualify — is this a real buyer, is there budget, is there a reason to act now — and book a meeting.
AEAccount Executive
Takes the qualified prospect — now an SQL, Sales Qualified Lead — and runs the actual sale: demos, proposals, negotiation, close. Carries the quota. This is whose time the job description wants to protect, and the person Gong is recording.
Customer SuccessTerry's team
After the deal is signed (closed-won), keeps the customer: onboarding, renewals, and selling them more (expansion). Their enemy is churn — customers leaving. The health-scoring and churn-prediction bullet in the posting is aimed here.

So when the posting says the AI SDR agent must “outperform the bottom half of the human BDR bench”: the bench is simply the roster of BDRs on staff, and the bar is that the agent should book more qualified meetings than their weaker half. It is a deliberately modest, measurable target — and the fact that they set it that precisely is a good sign about how they think.

An AI SDR agent, concretely

What you would build

Software that does the SDR job end to end: researches an account (headcount, tech stack, recent news, hiring signals), decides whether it matches the target profile, writes genuinely personalised outreach rather than mail-merge, sends it through the sequencing tool across several channels over days or weeks, reads and classifies the replies, answers the easy ones, and books the meeting on an AE's calendar — writing every touch back to the CRM as it goes.

Homework before you record: spend ten minutes finding the two or three AI SDR vendors people are actually deploying right now and skim what they claim. That market moves fast enough that naming a stale one is worse than naming none, so get it current yourself rather than relying on a list. Being able to say “I would run a bakeoff between X and Y” matches the posting's own structured-bakeoff requirement exactly.

The rest of the vocabulary

Glossary
ICP
Ideal Customer Profile — the written description of who you should target. Flosum's signals: Salesforce team size, current DevOps tooling, compliance requirements.
Pipeline
The total value of open deals in progress. “Pipeline generation” is the first of their three stated problems.
Win rate
Share of qualified opportunities that become customers. Theirs is stated at 20%.
ASP / ACV / “ASD”
Average Selling Price and Annual Contract Value — average deal size. The posting writes “$75K ASD,” almost certainly a typo for ASP. Understand it, do not correct them.
Sales cycle
Time from first contact to closed-won. Compressing it is worth as much as adding leads.
Ramp
How long a new rep takes to become productive. The implicit argument for agents: they ramp in days, not quarters.
Sequence / cadence
A scripted multi-step, multi-channel series of touches over days or weeks. Outreach and Salesloft exist to run these.
Speed-to-lead
How fast someone responds to an inbound enquiry. Minutes matter enormously, which is what “no lead waits” means.
PLG
Product-Led Growth — users try the product themselves before talking to sales. Their PLG motion is described as still maturing.
CRM hygiene
Keeping Salesforce records accurate and current. Universally hated admin work, and the single biggest drain on the AE time the role must reclaim.
ARR
Annual Recurring Revenue. Flosum states roughly $4M — small, which is why there is no support staff.
DORA
EU Digital Operational Resilience Act — financial-sector operational-resilience rules. Cited as an ICP signal because it forces regulated Salesforce customers to buy exactly what Flosum sells.

Their pipeline math is not hypothetical

Do this one out loud

The posting gives you a worked example: “we need 67 SQLs per quarter at 20% win rate and $75K ASD.” It is framed as an illustration of the reasoning they want. Run the arithmetic and it stops being an illustration.

67 SQLs × 20% win rate  =  13.4 closed-won deals per quarter
13.4 deals × $75,000  =  $1.005M per quarter
$1.005M × 4 quarters  =  ~$4.0M per year

Which is exactly the ~$4M ARR they state elsewhere in the same posting.

Those are not made-up teaching numbers. That is their actual business, and the arithmetic closes to the dollar. Saying so in one of your answers demonstrates the precise skill the requirement asks for — working backward from a revenue target — while also proving you read carefully. It is the single highest-return sentence available to you on this whole page.

Then show where AI moves each term, because that is the actual question underneath:

  • More SQLs into the top. Their own target is 10× personalised outbound volume without proportional headcount — that is the numerator.
  • Speed-to-lead on inbound. Real-time capture, scoring and routing so nothing sits in a queue. Cheapest conversion gain available to almost any company.
  • Higher conversion per stage. The closed-loop feedback bullet: let actual outcomes retrain targeting and messaging weekly instead of guessing.
  • More selling hours. Moving AE revenue-generating time from ~25% to 50%+ is a doubling of effective sales capacity at zero headcount — arguably the largest single number in the posting.
  • Protect the base. Churn prediction defends revenue you already have, which at $4M ARR is worth more than any new-logo improvement.

Your actual edge

Lead with this

Read these requirements again, with the agentic-platform work in mind. The posting asks for “AI agent orchestration — managing multiple AI agents working different segments or functions simultaneously, resolving conflicts, preventing duplicate outreach, and maintaining data quality in the hub” and for “prompt libraries, training datasets, and refinement workflows — treating agent training as a discipline, not a one-time setup.”

That is a description of running an agent fleet, written by someone who has been burned by one. Preventing duplicate outreach is a coordination problem — two agents claiming the same account is the same defect as two agents editing the same file. Maintaining data quality in the hub is write-conflict discipline. Most applicants for this role will be GTM operators who have configured a vendor tool; very few will have built the coordination layer underneath.

“The part of this I have actually lived is the orchestration layer. Once you have more than one agent working the same shared state, the interesting problems stop being about the prompt and start being about coordination — who owns this record, what happens when two agents reach the same account, how you keep the hub clean when several writers are working at once. You end up needing claims and ownership, not just better instructions.

And the failure mode that costs you is never obvious breakage. It is an agent that reports success and is quietly wrong — which in this context means a confidently personalised email sent to the wrong person at the wrong account. That is why I would not measure an SDR agent on volume. I would measure it on reply quality and on how much human correction each meeting booked actually cost.”

Their own line: bad AI automation destroys deals faster than no automation

Questions the ten deliverables imply

Prepare first sentences
Walk me through your first 30 days.
They have written the answer for you: one AI SDR agent deployed and personally trained, on either inbound qualification or outbound. Pick one and justify the pick — inbound is faster to show a number because the traffic already exists and speed-to-lead is the easiest win. Then describe the weekly refinement loop, because “continuously optimize” is in the title of the role.
How do you know the agent is working?
The bar is stated: beat the bottom half of the human BDR bench. Name the metrics — qualified meetings booked, reply rate, meetings that survive to a real opportunity, and the correction cost per booking. Volume alone is the wrong metric and saying so is a differentiator.
How would you pick a vendor?
They have prescribed a method: no more than two vendors per use case, structured bakeoff, reference checks by email, a conversation with a forward-deployed engineer before signing, and a 30-day training commitment. Echo the structure and add what you would measure in the bakeoff — same account list, same window, compare qualified meetings, not activity.
When should AI not replace a human here?
An explicit requirement, and the one where judgment shows. Late-stage deals, anything with pricing or contract terms, and any at-risk account — the cost of a wrong automated touch scales with deal stage. Automate the top of the funnel and the admin; keep humans where the relationship is.
Why do you want this / why Flosum?
Be concrete. A team at ~$4M ARR, reporting to a technical CEO, where you can ship and see the revenue number move within a quarter — that is a genuinely different proposition from the same work inside a large company. The Salesforce-ecosystem angle is real too: their buyers are DevOps and compliance people, which is a technical audience you can speak to honestly.

The gap — name it, do not paper over it

Honesty is the play

“Hands-on deploying and training AI SDR agents in a real B2B sales environment”

The posting is unusually explicit: “not evaluating, not advising, actually deploying and iterating weekly for at least 90 days.” If you have not done that in a revenue organisation, do not imply that you have. A CEO who wrote that sentence will test it in the next conversation, and being caught costs you more than the gap does.

The honest framing is also the stronger one: you have built the layer underneath these tools — multi-agent orchestration, conflict resolution, evaluation and refinement as an ongoing discipline — which is the scarcer half. What you would need to pick up is the GTM stack and the pipeline math, and you can demonstrate the second one already, in this interview, using their own 67-SQL example.

Say it in roughly that order: what you have not done, what you have done that transfers, and how fast you would close the difference. Then stop — do not over-explain a gap on camera. In a one-way video, a candidate who talks past the end of an answer sounds unsure of it.

The Palantir field decides this

Pick one

“Forward Deployed Engineer” is a Palantir-coined job title. A staffing firm advertising a Senior FDE with a Palantir-certification field is almost certainly standing up a Foundry practice for a client. That one line is the screen; everything else is qualification.

If you have it

Be specific rather than affirmative. Name the platform — Foundry or Gotham — the modules you actually touched (Pipeline Builder, Ontology, Workshop, Code Repositories), the duration, and whether you were deployed to a client or working internally.

“~2 years on Foundry — Pipeline Builder and Ontology, deployed onto two client engagements. No formal certification.”

If you don’t

Say so plainly and pivot in the same breath. FDE practices do hire adjacent backgrounds, because the scarce half of the role is embedded client delivery, not platform syntax. Hedging reads far worse than the gap itself and gets discovered anyway.

“No direct Palantir platform experience. My background is the same shape of work — embedded delivery of production data and AI systems against enterprise systems of record — and I’d expect to ramp on Foundry quickly, but I’d rather be straight with you about it than have it surface later.”

What a Forward Deployed Engineer actually is

The role shape

Worth knowing before any call, because the title misleads people into preparing as a platform engineer. An FDE is an engineer who is deployed to the customer — physically or virtually embedded with their team — to make a platform produce a result inside that customer’s messy reality. The work is roughly half engineering and half consulting: sit with the client, find out what they actually need as opposed to what the statement of work says, wire the platform into their systems of record, and ship something that survives after you leave.

That is why the screening list pairs Python and Kubernetes with “customer-facing” twice. The failure mode they are screening against is a strong engineer who cannot sit in a room with a frustrated client. The 25% travel is the tell — this role goes to the customer.

Compensation

Do not leave it blank

A range with a basis — not a number, not a dodge

Bulk screens discard non-answers, so “negotiable” or a blank is a real risk here. But a single number anchors you before you know the scope. The middle path is a range tied to something: “Targeting $X–$Y base depending on scope and travel load.”

The range has to come from your own current compensation and what you have seen for FDE roles in your market — it is not a number anyone can pick for you. Note that this is a staffing placement, so the band may be set by the end client’s contract rather than by Rackspace, which is another reason to ask for the JD before committing to a figure.

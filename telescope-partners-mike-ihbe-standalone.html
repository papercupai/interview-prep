01 · Before you dial

Three answers only you can give. Settle them first.

These come up in almost every first call with a small firm. A hesitant answer to any of them costs more than a weak technical answer.

On-site

Both posts say “On-site US, San Francisco.” The office is in the Presidio (1 Letterman Dr). If he asks, give a straight yes, no, or “yes, from [month].” Don't guess about hybrid work; ask him instead (see “Questions to ask”).

Comp

Your number

A tiny firm can be flexible, but it won't have a salary band doc. Have a base range ready, and know whether you care about carry or co-invest. At a VC firm those are real levers, and it's reasonable to ask whether engineers get them.

Why here

Why a VC firm and not a startup?

A good honest answer: the whole company is the customer. You'd sit next to the people whose work you're automating and see every week whether it worked. That's the loop you built Papercusp around, only with investors instead of coding agents.

04 · Read the post closely

What the job actually is

The June post asked for a “Software Engineer” to improve “team efficiency.” The September post changed the title to “Software Engineer & Data Scientist” and added “improving investment decision-making using data.” So data is now half the job. Expect questions about it.

Sourcing

Finding companies before they're obvious

Their thesis is “post product-market fit but not yet obvious,” which is a signals problem: hiring velocity, headcount growth, web traffic, product launches, customer logos, review counts, GitHub activity. The pipeline is ingest, entity resolution, enrich, score, then partner review and a write back to the CRM.

Diligence

Memos and meeting prep

Agents draft company briefs, market maps and competitor tables before a partner call, and pull a first-pass memo from the data room. Every claim should be cited so a partner can check it in a few seconds. This very page is a small example.

Portfolio

Monitoring and ops

Collect monthly portfolio metrics, flag drift, prepare board decks and handle LP reporting. This work is dull and repetitive, and it's where automation saves the most time.

07 · What he'll probably ask

Likely questions, with answer sketches

Design

“How would you automate our deal sourcing?”

Start with the output: a weekly ranked list a partner trusts, with a “why now” line and citations. Then work backward. Sources (Crunchbase/PitchBook/Harmonic, LinkedIn headcount, job posts, web traffic) go through entity resolution into one company table in Postgres. Next come time-series signals, a score, a partner review queue and a write back to the CRM. The key is the feedback loop: log every partner pass or pursue as a label.

Data science

“Could you build a model that picks winners?”

Be honest: 22 investments is far too few to train on outcomes. Use proxy labels such as “raised a next round within 18 months,” measured across thousands of companies. Treat it as a ranking problem, not classification. Watch for leakage (features measured after the round) and survivorship (dead companies drop out of the data). Check it with a time-split backtest: would it have surfaced Telescope's actual deals before they closed?

Trust

“How do you know the AI output is right?”

Every claim is cited to a source so it can be checked quickly. Keep a fixed eval set of past memos with known answers, and re-run it whenever you change the prompt or model. The model that writes the work shouldn't grade it. Track which claims partners correct and treat those as bugs. This is Answer Bank #8, “Evaluating AI output,” so use those numbers.

Adoption

“What if the partners don't use it?”

Then it's a product failure, not a user failure. Put it where they already work: the CRM, email, the Monday meeting doc. Ship one small workflow that saves a partner an hour a week, measure whether they keep using it, then expand. Don't build a new dashboard nobody opens.

Stack

“What do you work in?”

TypeScript and Node, Postgres (920 migrations; state lives in PG by default), Python for data work, LLM APIs (Anthropic and OpenAI), MCP tools and Tauri for the desktop app. Be specific about how much SQL and pandas-style analysis you've actually done. The post now says “data scientist.”

Behavioral

“Tell me about something you got wrong”

Use Answer Bank #6, “Being wrong.” A story about a confident, reasonable diagnosis that turned out wrong, and the guardrail you added afterward, fits a Sentry person well.

08 · Your turn

Questions to ask Mike

This shows you the roadmap and what he thinks is most important.

Team

This tells you how much you'd own and who you'd learn from.

Data

“What's the data stack: which CRM, which data vendors, and is there a warehouse yet?”

Affinity, Harmonic and PitchBook are common in VC. This tells you whether you're building the foundation or working on top of one.

Change

This shows you read both posts and gets at what they need most right now.

Adoption

“How do Mickey and the other partners feel about automating the ‘intelligence’ part?”

The real limit is whether partners trust and use it. Find out how willing they are.

Success

“What would make the first six months a clear win?”

Then ask about next steps and whether there's a take-home or an onsite.

Also ask about: how hybrid the on-site setup really is, whether engineers get carry or co-invest, and whether the tools will also be offered to portfolio companies.
